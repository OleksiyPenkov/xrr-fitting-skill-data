**2. My "missing cobalt" conclusion was wrong, and this is the important correction.** The measured critical angle, 0.259° at the half-height of the plateau, requires a mean film density of ~3.3 g/cm³ — far above carbon. Doing the mass balance on the final fit: the Co layer (1.88 Å at ρ 9.0) plus the cobalt dissolved in the carbon layer (which refines to ρ 2.895 against the cap's 1.86, implying ~15% Co by volume, ~3.9 Å) gives **~5.7 Å of cobalt per period against 5.58 Å deposited — about 103%**. The cobalt all arrives. It simply does not stay layered: σ is 7.6–9.5 Å, comparable to the layer spacing, so the modulation is weak and the Bragg peak is small. That is interdiffusion, not loss, and it is a far better situation for the mirror than what I reported.
